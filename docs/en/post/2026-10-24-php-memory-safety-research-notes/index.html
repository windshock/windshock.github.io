PHP Memory-Safety Research Notes (2016–2026): Discoverers, Root Causes, Fixes and Evidence

A source-based provenance map of PHP memory-safety findings from 2016 to 2026, crediting original researchers and maintainers while separating issue status, reproducibility, exploitability, and patch verification.

These notes document sources and evidence behind A Decade of PHP Memory-Safety Bugs. Their purpose is to credit prior work accurately, connect discoveries to root-cause families and fixes, and avoid conflating a report with confirmed impact or verified patch effectiveness. The source-review cutoff is October 24, 2026; live issue and pull-request states may have changed.

The underlying research surveyed original security write-ups, PHP’s public bug database and GitHub issues, security advisories, PHP Foundation reports, core developer discussions, and the FlowFusion paper. A selected set of 58 historical records is maintained in windshock/php2shell’s corpus work. Fifty-eight records do not mean 58 independently verified, still-vulnerable bugs, and the examples below are not a statistically representative sample of all PHP vulnerabilities.

Evidence vocabulary#

LevelWhat it establishesRequired provenance
ReportedA researcher filed a report or published a technical accountOriginal author, date, source URL
ReachedExecution reached a relevant site or transitionCoverage, breakpoint, state event
TriggeredThe relevant failure condition or invariant violation occurredFault-site or state-transition record
DetectedA sanitizer, debugger, assertion, or equivalent observer detected a problemTool output, version, PID, build provenance
Attackability assessedAttack conditions and impact were evaluatedOriginal research or official advisory, SAPI/context
Patch verifiedThe same path was contrasted before and after a validated fixExact commit/release and two valid executions

Open does not establish that the latest supported release remains exploitable. Closed does not, by itself, establish that all maintained branches contain a fix. Likewise, a crash under a locally executed PHP program does not necessarily imply a remotely exploitable HTTP vulnerability.

The Quarkslab audit and the later 2026 security response#

The PHP Foundation’s 2025 audit summary describes the Quarkslab core review. Secondary summaries, including OSTIF’s publication, may categorize severities somewhat differently. Before republishing a precise severity breakdown, it is better to reconcile individual findings against the final audit revision.

The Foundation’s October 2026 account describes compatibility, ecosystem impact, availability, and decision-process difficulties in reaching an OOM-related security fix. An earlier audit finding, a warning signal recognized during remediation, and a later concrete CVE should not be collapsed into the same discovery.

From historical records to falsifiable experiments#

A research corpus becomes more valuable when each historical record contains:

  • Fix lineage: original source and report date, fix PR/commit, affected versions, and release branches carrying the fix.
  • Execution prerequisites: PHP version, build/allocator, SAPI, extensions, and whether same-worker execution matters.
  • An appropriate observer: ASan, MSan, GDB/watchpoint, Valgrind, or a PHP-level GC invariant, along with whether that observer actually ran.
  • Independent evidence levels: reached, triggered, detected, attackability assessed, and patch verified.
  • Evaluation independence: a public regression set for already-understood defects versus a separate, preselected, tool-frozen blind hold-out set.

For instance, #78793 motivates an MSan-positive comparator, #76167 a worker-lifetime watchpoint, #10026 a GC-invariant test, and #76846 / #12073 a failure-atomicity before/after comparison. An observer that was never executed cannot contribute a negative test result.

The Magma benchmark motivates separating reach, trigger, and detection; StateAFL motivates internal server-state observations; and FuzzBench motivates controlled comparisons and independent repetitions. These are methodological references, not third-party confirmations of a particular php2shell result.

Scope, limitations, and related resources#

These notes are a source-based research map, not a claim that all 58 corpus entries have been independently reproduced on every release or that an open issue proves current exploitability. They distinguish public-source interpretation from observations already documented in the laboratory; no additional full PHP-FPM/ASan campaign was run solely for this article.
